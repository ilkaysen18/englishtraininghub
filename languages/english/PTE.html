About PTE Academic Speaking

PTE Academic is a computer-based, computer-assessed test of English. The Speaking & Writing section is Part 1 of the exam (77-93 minutes). Unlike IELTS, there is no human examiner — you speak into a microphone and responses are scored by AI.

Personal Introduction

Not Scored

Read the prompt and give some information about yourself. You have 25 seconds to read the prompt, then 30 seconds to record your response. This is sent to institutions along with your score report but is not scored.

Tips

  • Speak clearly and at a natural pace — don't rush.
  • Cover all points mentioned in the prompt.
  • This is your first impression to the institution, so be confident!

Practice Prompts:

Read Aloud

Scored

A text of up to 60 words appears on screen. You have 30-40 seconds to prepare, then you must read the text aloud. You are scored on content, oral fluency, and pronunciation.

Tips

  • Use preparation time to identify difficult words and plan your intonation.
  • Speak at a steady pace — don't speed up or slow down unnaturally.
  • Pay attention to word stress and sentence rhythm.
  • Don't go back and correct yourself; continue smoothly.

Practice Passages:

Repeat Sentence

Scored

You hear a sentence (3-9 seconds long). After the recording stops, repeat the sentence exactly as you heard it. Scored on content, oral fluency, and pronunciation.

Tips

  • Focus on key content words first, then filler words.
  • Shadow the rhythm and intonation you hear.
  • If you forget a word, keep going — don't pause too long.
  • Practice by reading each sentence, covering it, and repeating from memory.

Practice Sentences:

Describe Image

Scored

An image (graph, chart, table, map, or diagram) appears on screen. You have 25 seconds to study it, then 40 seconds to describe what you see. Scored on content, oral fluency, and pronunciation.

Tips

  • Start with an overview: "This image shows / This graph illustrates..."
  • Identify the key features, trends, and any significant data points.
  • Use comparison language: higher, lower, increased, decreased, similar.
  • Conclude with a summary statement if time allows.

Practice Scenarios:

Re-tell Lecture

Scored

You hear or watch a lecture (up to 90 seconds). After it finishes, you have 10 seconds to prepare, then 40 seconds to re-tell the lecture in your own words. Scored on content, oral fluency, and pronunciation.

Tips

  • Take brief notes while listening — focus on main idea, key points, and examples.
  • Structure your re-telling: introduction, main points, conclusion.
  • Use linking words: firstly, moreover, in addition, finally.
  • Paraphrase where possible — don't try to memorize exact words.

Practice Lectures:

Answer Short Question

Scored

You hear a question (3-9 seconds). Answer with a single word or a few words. You have 10 seconds to respond. Scored on vocabulary.

Tips

  • Answer concisely — one word or a short phrase is enough.
  • Don't overthink; the answer is usually a common English word.
  • Questions test general knowledge and vocabulary, not specialist knowledge.
  • Click "Show Answer" to check your response after attempting each question.
